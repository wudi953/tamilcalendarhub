About This Site

A small, practical reference for anyone who needs Tamil festival dates in English — built from published panchangam data and calculated for Chennai.

Who I Am

I am the person who built and maintains Tamil Calendar 2027 — a regular visitor who got tired of hunting through half a dozen sites to answer a simple question: "what is the Tamil date today?" I grew up around the Tamil calendar, and I still check a panchangam before any family event. This is a personal project, not an institution, a temple or a government publication. I write the pages, check the dates, and keep the calendar grids printable so that anyone can use them without signing up for anything.

Why I Built It

Most Tamil calendar information online is either in Tamil, locked inside a PDF, or scattered across forums and social media posts. That is fine if you already know where to look, but unhelpful for students, new parents, people planning a wedding, and members of the Tamil diaspora who want a quick English explanation alongside the date. I wanted one place where each month and each major festival has a clear page: the date, the Tamil month, the customs, the food, and practical notes about travel and temples. Everything is written in plain English, works on a phone, and prints cleanly on A4 so it can go on a wall or into a diary.

The site also tries to explain the "why" behind the dates. Tamil festivals follow a solar calendar and a lunar tithi system that works differently from the Gregorian calendar, which is why dates move each year and why Tamil Deepavali and North Indian Diwali can fall on different days. A date without that context is easy to misread, so the pages include short explanations rather than bare tables.

Where the Dates Come From

Festival and muhurtham information on this site is checked against published panchangam sources, including Drik Panchang, and cross-referenced with traditional Tamil almanac conventions. All dates are calculated in Indian Standard Time (IST) using Chennai as the reference location — sunrise, tithi endings and therefore festival days can differ by a day in other parts of India, Sri Lanka, Singapore or Malaysia.

That Chennai baseline is deliberate: it gives one consistent reference point for every page on the site. It also means a date shown here may not match what your local temple or family panchangam announces, especially near sunrise boundaries. Treat these pages as a starting point, and always confirm important dates — weddings, housewarmings, temple visits — with your own priest, temple office or a local panchangam. Government holiday lists are compiled from state announcements and should be checked against the official gazette once published.

Keeping the Site Honest

When a date is corrected or a holiday is gazetted, I update the relevant page rather than quietly leaving an old version online. If you spot something that looks wrong, the contact page is the fastest way to tell me.
