Tamil Calendar 2028 April

April is the turning point of the Tamil year. Panguni gives way to Chithirai on the 14th, and the month carries two confirmed dates: Panguni Uthiram on the 8th and Puthandu, the Tamil New Year, on the 14th. It is one of the busiest weeks for family gatherings and temple visits.

Festivals in April 2028

  • Saturday, 8 April — Panguni Uthiram
    Panguni Uthiram falls on the full-moon day of the Tamil month Panguni and is observed across Tamil Nadu with temple processions, special abhishekam and offerings. The day is associated with divine weddings in temple tradition, and devotees gather at large temples to watch the festival image processions in the evening. Households keep a quiet fast or a simple meal and visit the local temple with flowers and lamps.
  • Friday, 14 April — Puthandu (Tamil New Year)
    Puthandu is the first day of Chithirai and the start of the Tamil year. Families draw a fresh kolam, hang mango leaves at the doorway and lay out the kanni — a tray of fruits, gold, betel leaves and a mirror — to be seen first thing in the morning for good luck. Temples hold special pujas, and the day is a public holiday in Tamil Nadu. Full Tamil New Year guide →

Government Holidays in April 2028

Puthandu on 14 April is expected to be a Tamil Nadu state holiday, and many employers also allow a day around it, which often creates a short new-year break. No national holiday falls in April. Banks, schools and government offices follow the state list, while private companies set their own policies. The official gazette is published closer to the year and is the final authority, so treat this as a planning guide until the list is confirmed.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Chithirai, which begins with Puthandu on 14 April, is regarded as one of the most auspicious stretches of the Tamil year. The clean start of the new calendar makes the weeks that follow popular for property registrations, business openings and wedding planning. This page does not list muhurtham timings: the right moment depends on the family chart, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam. Ask a family priest before fixing an important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

April closes Panguni (பங்குனி) and, from the 14th, opens Chithirai (சித்திரை), the first month of the Tamil year. The name is linked to the Chitra star and is often read as picture or beauty, a fitting image for a dry, bright month freshly decorated for the new year. Puthandu marks more than a date: it is the point where the solar calendar restarts, so Chithirai carries the idea of a clean slate, new account books and beginning again.

Festival Customs and Food

On Puthandu, households tie mango-leaf garlands over the doorway, draw a new kolam and perform a bhoomi pooja asking the earth for a productive year. The signature dish is mango pachadi, a balanced mix of raw mango, jaggery, chilli, salt, turmeric and neem said to represent the six flavours of life; payasam and dosa follow. Panguni Uthiram is quieter at home, with most of the celebration taking place in the temple during the evening procession.

Travel and Temple Notes

The Puthandu week is a busy travel period, with families returning home or visiting relatives around the 14th. Trains and buses toward Madurai, Trichy and other temple towns fill early, so book ahead if possible. Temples are crowded for both Panguni Uthiram and Puthandu; arrive early, remove footwear at the entrance and follow the queue. The afternoon heat is already building, so plan the main darshan in the morning and carry water.
