Auspicious Days and Notes

Aani, the first half of July, is a quiet stretch in which families often arrange weddings and small household ceremonies, while Aadi takes over around the 14th and is associated in popular tradition with new clothes and household purchases. No muhurtham timings are listed here: an auspicious moment depends on the family chart, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam. Ask a priest before fixing any important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

July is shared by Aani (ஆனி) and Aadi (ஆடி). Aani takes its name from the star Anusham and is often read as a month of growth, when the heat gives way to the first rains and water levels begin to rise. Aadi, which starts around the 14th, is widely associated with Amman worship, new clothes and the practice of buying household essentials in the sale season. The handover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed for 2028; this boundary is approximate.

Seasonal Customs and Temple Routine

With no festival date confirmed for July 2028, July is shaped by the season and the regular temple week. The southwest monsoon starts to reach Tamil Nadu, so mornings feel heavier and temple visits are commonly planned early. Mondays draw devotees for Shiva abhishekam, Fridays for Amman lamps, and neighbourhood shrines hold their usual weekly pujas rather than a large annual event. At home, the month is a practical one: new clothes are bought, kolams are kept fresh, and families visit temples without the crowds of a festival day.

Travel and Temple Notes

July travel in Tamil Nadu can be affected by monsoon showers, especially along the coast, so leave extra time for road journeys and check weather before a long drive. With no festival confirmed for July 2028, queues are usually ordinary and buses or trains can often be booked at short notice. Carry an umbrella, remove footwear at the temple entrance, and follow local notices about maintenance work or small processions.
