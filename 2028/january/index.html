Festivals in January 2028

  • Friday, 14 January — Bhogi
    Bhogi opens the Pongal season and marks the last day of Margazhi. Families clean their homes, draw fresh kolam at the entrance, and burn old mats and discarded clothes in a small bonfire before sunrise. The fire symbolises letting go of what is worn out and making room for a fresh start. Neighbours often gather around the bonfire together, and the day ends with a shared meal.
  • Saturday, 15 January — Pongal (Thai Pongal)
    Pongal, also called Thai Pongal, is the harvest festival and the first day of the Tamil month Thai. Families boil new rice with milk and jaggery in a decorated clay pot until it overflows, a sign of abundance, then offer the first serving to the sun god. Sugarcane, fresh turmeric and a new kolam complete the scene. Full Pongal guide →
  • Sunday, 16 January — Mattu Pongal
    Mattu Pongal honours cattle, the animals that have supported farming through the year. Farmers bathe and paint the horns of their cows and bulls, garland them, and feed them a meal of the same new rice prepared for Pongal. In villages around Madurai and elsewhere, jallikattu events draw large crowds. The day is a public thank-you to the livestock that make the harvest possible.
  • Monday, 17 January — Uzhavar Thirunal
    Uzhavar Thirunal, the farmers' day, closes the Pongal cluster of holidays. It recognises the work of the people who cultivate the land, and in Tamil Nadu it is observed as a state holiday alongside Pongal and Mattu Pongal. Families often use the extra day to visit relatives, attend village events or rest after the busy festival days.

Government Holidays in January 2028

The Pongal holidays form a three-day break in Tamil Nadu: Pongal on 15 January, Mattu Pongal on 16 January and Uzhavar Thirunal on 17 January. Republic Day on 26 January is a national holiday. Banks, schools and government offices follow the state list, while private employers may close for fewer days. The official gazette must be published before the holiday list can be confirmed; until then, treat these as expected dates.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Thai, which begins on 15 January with Pongal, is traditionally regarded as an auspicious month for new beginnings. Families often plan engagements, property registrations and other milestones in the weeks that follow, and temple calendars fill with special pujas. This page deliberately does not list muhurtham timings: an auspicious moment depends on the person's chart, the weekday, the tithi and the local panchangam, so a generic time slot would be misleading. Ask a family priest or consult a full panchangam before fixing an important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

January spans two Tamil months. Margazhi (மார்கழி), which runs until the 14th, is associated with devotion and the early-morning singing of hymns that fills temples before dawn. Its name is commonly linked to the idea of a sacred path. Thai (தை), beginning on Pongal day, is understood as the fresh or pure month and is one of the most favoured stretches of the Tamil year. The change from a quiet, devotional Margazhi to a celebratory Thai is one of the sharpest turns in the calendar.

Festival Customs and Food

Each day of the Pongal season has its own ritual and its own food. On Bhogi, old belongings are burnt before sunrise and the house is cleaned for the year ahead. Pongal itself is sweet rice boiled with milk and jaggery until the pot overflows; ven pongal, sugarcane, turmeric and payasam are shared with visitors. Mattu Pongal turns attention to cattle, which are washed, decorated and fed the same new rice. Together the three days turn the harvest into a family celebration.

Travel and Temple Notes

Pongal is one of the heaviest travel periods of the Tamil year. City workers return to their villages in the days before 15 January, so buses and trains should be booked well in advance, and temple towns fill early. Margazhi temple visits begin before sunrise and are best planned with warm clothing. During the festival, verify processions and road closures locally, remove footwear at temple entrances, and follow the queues. Allow extra time for every journey across the Pongal week.
