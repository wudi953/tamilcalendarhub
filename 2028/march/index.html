Tamil Calendar 2028 March

March carries the Tamil year from Masi into Panguni, a stretch when the weather turns warmer and the urban calendar is still relatively quiet. No festival date has been confirmed for 2028 in this month, so the grid below has no festival marks. The significance of March is mostly seasonal: fields are settling after the harvest, mango trees flower, and temple committees begin preparing for the celebrations that arrive with the new Tamil year in April.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Masi, the first half of March, is a month associated with Shiva in Tamil tradition; Mondays in particular are kept as days of prayer and lamp offerings. Panguni takes over around the 14th and is widely regarded as favourable for weddings and new ventures, with its own full-moon observance later in the month. No specific muhurtham times are listed here. The right moment depends on the couple's charts, the weekday and the local panchangam, so families should ask their priest rather than rely on a general guide.

What the Tamil Months Mean

March is split between Masi (மாசி) and Panguni (பங்குனி). Masi is linked in popular explanation to the moon and to the darker fortnight of the lunar month, and its temple observances are largely Shaiva. Panguni takes its name from the Uttaram star and is often read as the month of new growth, when the heat rises and trees flower. The handover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the Thai and Chithirai starts are fixed in the 2028 data; the Panguni boundary is approximate.

Travel and Temple Notes

March weather in Tamil Nadu is warm by afternoon but still comfortable in the early morning, which suits temple visits. Without a major festival in the confirmed list, queues are ordinary and road closures are unlikely, so this is a practical month for a short temple circuit or a family outing. Carry water and a hat for midday, and plan the main darshan before the heat builds. Coastal routes can be windy as the kite season picks up, so check conditions before a long drive.
