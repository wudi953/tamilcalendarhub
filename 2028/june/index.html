Tamil Calendar 2028 June

June moves the Tamil calendar from Vaikasi into Aani, the month that leads toward the mid-year Aadi season. No festival date is confirmed for the month in the current 2028 data, so the grid carries no marks. The southwest monsoon begins to reach Tamil Nadu, temples continue their regular weekly pujas, and the month is a gap before the heavier festival calendar of later in the year.

Final leave planning should wait for the Tamil Nadu gazette, issued closer to the year, which overrides any guide; this page records only what is confirmed today.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Vaikasi, the first half of June, is traditionally associated with temple worship and acts of charity, while Aani takes over around the 14th and is often read as a month of growth as the monsoon approaches. Families may still use these weeks for weddings and small ceremonies, but no muhurtham timings are given here. The right moment depends on the couple's charts, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam, so ask a priest rather than rely on a general guide.

What the Tamil Months Mean

June is split between Vaikasi (வைகாசி) and Aani (ஆனி). Vaikasi follows the new year and is linked in popular explanation with visiting temples and giving alms. Aani, which begins around the 14th, takes its name from the star Anusham and is often associated with the onset of rains and the swelling of rivers and tanks. The handover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed for 2028; this boundary is approximate.

Travel and Temple Notes

June can bring the first monsoon showers to Tamil Nadu, so travel plans should allow for rain and the occasional disruption on coastal roads. Temperatures are slightly lower than in May, but the humidity rises, especially near the coast. With no major festival in the confirmed list, temple queues are usually ordinary and booking buses or trains at short notice is often possible. Check local weather before a long drive, and remove footwear at temple entrances as usual.
