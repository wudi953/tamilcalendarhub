Nothing in the confirmed 2028 data creates a state holiday in September, and there is no national holiday to note. Government offices, banks and schools are expected to keep their normal schedule. Private firms generally follow the same pattern, though some allow a floating leave around local temple events. Final leave planning should wait for the Tamil Nadu gazette, published closer to the year, which overrides any guide; this page records only what is confirmed today.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Aavani, the first half of September, is often read as a month in which responsibilities and relationships grow. Purattasi, which begins around the 14th, is regarded as favourable for devotional practice, and many families keep a regular Saturday routine of temple visits and fasting. No muhurtham timings are listed here: an auspicious moment depends on the family chart, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam. Ask a priest before fixing any important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

September is divided between Aavani (ஆவணி) and Purattasi (புரட்டாசி). Aavani takes its name from the star Avani and is commonly understood as the month in which bonds deepen. Purattasi, which starts around the 14th, derives its name from the star Pooram and is closely identified with Vishnu worship and Saturday temple visits in Tamil households. The handover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed in the 2028 data; this boundary is approximate.

Seasonal Customs and Temple Routine

September is shaped by the monsoon and the weekly temple calendar rather than by a dated festival. Saturdays draw larger crowds to Vishnu temples, where devotees offer tulasi and listen to the day's puja, while the other days keep the usual morning and evening schedule. Rain is common, so plan darshan early and carry an umbrella. At home the routine is quiet: lamps are lit in the evening, the kolam is renewed, and families begin thinking about the deeper temple observances of the closing months.

Travel and Temple Notes

Wet weather can slow traffic in September, especially on coastal roads and low-lying streets, so allow extra time for temple trips. With no major festival in the confirmed list, queues are generally ordinary and booking buses or trains at short notice is often possible. Carry an umbrella, remove footwear at the sanctum, and check local notices for maintenance or small processions before you travel.
