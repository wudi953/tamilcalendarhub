Auspicious Days and Notes

Karthigai, which continues until about the 13th, is a favoured time for weddings and housewarmings as the weather cools. Margazhi takes over around the 14th and is traditionally a month of devotion rather than celebration, with early-morning hymns and temple discipline. No muhurtham timings are listed here: an auspicious moment depends on the family chart, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam. Ask a priest before fixing any important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

December is split between Karthigai (கார்த்திகை) and Margazhi (மார்கழி). Karthigai takes its name from the Karthigai stars and is associated with the cooling weather and clearing skies of the north-east monsoon. Margazhi, which begins around the 14th, is linked in popular explanation with a sacred path and is known for devotional singing before dawn. The handover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed in the 2028 data; this boundary is approximate.

Seasonal Customs and Temple Routine

December is shaped by the devotional rhythm of Margazhi rather than by a dated festival. Temples open early for hymn singing, and families who observe the month keep a simple routine of waking before dawn, lighting a lamp and attending the morning recital. Evenings are quieter, with dinner taken early and visitors received in the cooler hours. The few weeks before the new year are a time for cleaning the house, renewing kolams and preparing for the festival season that returns with Thai.

Travel and Temple Notes

The cool mornings make December pleasant for temple visits, and the early-morning hymn sessions are the main draw. Accommodation in temple towns can fill during the devotional season, so book ahead if you plan an overnight stay. North-east monsoon rain is still possible, so check the forecast for long drives. Remove footwear at the sanctum, keep quiet during the recital, and follow the queue.
