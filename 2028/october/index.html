Tamil Calendar 2028 October

October is one of the busiest months of the 2028 Tamil calendar. Gandhi Jayanti falls on the 2nd as a national holiday, and Deepavali is confirmed for the 17th, with the Tamil Nadu date following the local panchangam. The month moves from Purattasi into Aippasi and builds steadily toward the festival week.

Government Holidays in October 2028

Gandhi Jayanti on 2 October is a national holiday, and Deepavali on 17 October is expected to be a Tamil Nadu state holiday. Banks, schools and government offices follow the official list, while private employers set their own policy and may add an extra day around the festival. The state gazette published closer to the year is the final authority for leave planning, so treat these as expected holidays until it appears.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Aippasi, which begins around the 14th, is counted among the favoured stretches of the Tamil year for weddings and housewarmings, and families often combine Deepavali visits with plans for the months ahead. This page does not list muhurtham timings: an auspicious moment depends on the family chart, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam. Ask a family priest before fixing an important date.

What the Tamil Months Mean

October closes Purattasi (புரட்டாசி) and, from about the 14th, opens Aippasi (ஐப்பசி). Purattasi takes its name from the Pooram star and is closely identified with Vishnu worship and Saturday temple visits. Aippasi is named after the Apam star and is often read as a month of easing heat and deepening devotional life, when the rains have settled and the year moves toward its closing festivals. The boundary is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed for 2028.

Festival Customs and Food

Deepavali preparations begin days ahead with adhirasam, murukku, thattai and mixture stored in sealed tins. On the morning itself, elders apply gingelly oil before the ritual bath, the family takes a first bite of herb-and-jaggery lehiyam, and everyone dresses in new clothes. Temples are crowded from morning, crackers build through the afternoon and evening, and lamps are lit after dusk. Families visit elders for blessings, and businesses mark the day by blessing new account books.

Travel and Temple Notes

Deepavali is one of the heaviest travel periods of the year. Trains, buses and flights to hometowns fill well in advance, and the days around the festival should be booked early. In the towns, firecracker sales and temporary stalls make some streets slow, so allow extra time for temple visits and follow local fire-safety notices. Arrive early for darshan, remove footwear at the entrance, and keep the evening journey flexible after the lamps are lit.
