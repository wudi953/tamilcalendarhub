Tamil Calendar 2028 August

August moves the Tamil calendar from Aadi into Aavani. The month's fixed date is Independence Day on the 15th, marked as a national holiday; no Tamil festival date has been announced for August. Temples keep their weekly rhythm, the monsoon brings intermittent rain, and the Aadi shopping season continues into the first half.

Festivals in August 2028

No 2028 Tamil festival dates are confirmed for this month yet. Independence Day on the 15th is a national holiday rather than a Tamil festival and is covered in the holiday section below.

Government Holidays in August 2028

Independence Day on 15 August is a national holiday, so banks, schools and government offices are expected to close. No Tamil Nadu state holiday is confirmed for August in the current 2028 data. Private employers usually give the national holiday but set their own policy for any additional day, and the official gazette published closer to the year is the final authority for leave planning.

Auspicious Days and Notes

Aadi, which continues until about the 13th, is associated with Amman temples and household buying, while Aavani takes over around the 14th and is often read as a time when bonds and responsibilities grow. Families may still use these weeks for weddings and small ceremonies, but no muhurtham timings are given here. The right moment depends on the couple's charts, the weekday and the tithi in the local panchangam, so ask a priest rather than rely on a general guide.

What the Tamil Months Mean

August is split between Aadi (ஆடி) and Aavani (ஆவணி). Aadi is widely associated with Amman worship, new clothes and the practice of buying household essentials during the sale season. Aavani, which begins around the 14th, takes its name from the star Avani and is commonly understood as the month in which relationships deepen and duties are renewed. The changeover is shown as till ~13 and from ~14 because only the starts of Thai on 15 January and Chithirai on 14 April are fixed for 2028; this boundary is approximate.

Seasonal Customs and Temple Routine

August is a month of regular temple life rather than one large dated festival. Monday abhishekam for Shiva and Friday lamp offerings for Amman continue as usual, and neighbourhood shrines hold their weekly pujas. Showers interrupt the heat, so early-morning darshan is the most comfortable time. At home, families finish the Aadi purchases, keep the entrance kolam fresh, and prepare for the heavier temple calendar that builds from September onward.

Travel and Temple Notes

Independence Day is a national holiday, so trains and buses can be busier than usual around the 15th; book ahead if you plan a trip. Monsoon showers may slow road travel, particularly near the coast, so check the weather before setting out. With no large festival crowd in the confirmed list, temple queues are generally manageable. Carry an umbrella, remove footwear at the entrance, and follow local notices at the shrine.
