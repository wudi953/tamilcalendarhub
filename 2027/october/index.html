Tamil Calendar 2027 October

October 2027 is a double-header: Navarathiri ends with Vijayadashami on 9 October, and the lights of Deepavali arrive on 28 October — plus Gandhi Jayanti on the 2nd.

Government Holidays in October 2027

Gandhi Jayanti on Saturday, 2 October is a national holiday. Vijayadashami on 9 October and Deepavali on 28 October are expected Tamil Nadu holidays — Deepavali usually brings a one- or two-day official break, and many companies extend it across the week. Train and bus bookings for the Deepavali weekend open early and sell out fast, so plan hometown travel as soon as dates are confirmed.

Auspicious Days and Notes for October 2027

Vijayadashami is one of the most auspicious days of the entire year — new ventures, new classes and first lessons (vidyarambam) are traditionally begun on this day, so it also sees heavy demand for muhurtham slots. After Deepavali, Aippasi carries the momentum into the wedding season that peaks in the following months. See our muhurtham dates guide for how auspicious timings are determined.

What the Tamil Months Mean

October begins in Purattasi (புரட்டாசி), the Navaratri month, and enters Aippasi (ஐப்பசி) around the 17th. Aippasi takes its name from the Ashvini nakshatra and is the month of Deepavali, the festival of lights. With the north-east monsoon arriving and the new rice coming in, Aippasi sits at a turning point: the year's hard field work is giving way to celebration, and the evenings grow longer and darker — exactly the setting in which rows of oil lamps make their strongest impression.

Festival Customs and Food

Deepavali is observed on Naraka Chathurdasi with a ritual oil bath before sunrise, after which everyone dresses in new clothes. Elders apply oil to younger family members, lamps are lit after dusk, and crackers mark the defeat of Narakasura. Tamil households prepare adhirasam, murukku, mixture and the herb-and-jaggery legiyam traditionally eaten after the bath; North Indian Diwali, a day later in 2027, centres on Lakshmi puja and sweets. Aippasi also includes Thula Snanam, a holy bath during the solar transition, observed with fasting and charity.

Season and Farming

The north-east monsoon is the defining weather event of the month. It brings the rain that Tamil Nadu depends on after the south-west monsoon, filling tanks and bringing the main harvest into view. Farmers check drainage, protect the standing crop from heavy downpours and begin cutting early paddy where it has ripened. The first showers also break the heat, making temple processions and evening street festivals comfortable again. Markets are full of new rice, sugarcane and the ingredients for Deepavali sweets.

Travel and Temple Notes

Deepavali is a major travel and shopping season. Roads around temple towns are crowded in the days before the festival, and cracker stalls occupy entire streets — follow the designated firing zones and keep children and pets at a safe distance. Oil lamps and open flames are common in homes and temples, so keep clothing and decorations clear. In Tamil Nadu the festival falls on the 28th while North Indian Diwali falls on the 29th in 2027, so a two-day celebration is normal in mixed families. Carry an umbrella for monsoon showers.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

Those born after the Aippasi transition fall under Thula (Libra, துலாம்), a rashi traditionally associated with balance, fairness and an ease with people. Thula natives are often described as diplomatic, aesthetically minded and careful about keeping relationships even — a fitting sign for a month that joins two communities' celebrations. The Purattasi days at the start of the month belong to Kanya (Virgo), associated with precision and service. These solar readings are broad sketches; the birth star and moon matter more in a full horoscope.
