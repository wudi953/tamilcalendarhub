Tamil Calendar 2027 January

January 2027 is the biggest festival month of the Tamil year: Margazhi ends, Thai begins, and the four-day Pongal season arrives with Bhogi, Pongal and Mattu Pongal, followed by Thai Pusam.

Festivals in January 2027

  • Thursday, 14 January — Bhogi (போகி)
    The first day of the Pongal festival. Homes are scrubbed and freshly painted, old mats and clothes are burnt on a small bonfire before sunrise, and doorsteps are decorated with new kolam. Margazhi, the month of devotional singing, ends on this day.
  • Friday, 15 January — Pongal / Thai Pongal (தைப்பொங்கல்)
    The harvest festival and the first day of Thai. Families boil new rice with jaggery and milk in a decorated clay pot until it overflows — a symbol of abundance. Sugarcane, fresh turmeric and kolam complete the scene. Full Pongal 2027 guide →
  • Saturday, 16 January — Mattu Pongal (மட்டுப்பொங்கல்)
    Cattle are bathed, painted and fed Pongal in thanks for their farm work. Jallikattu, the famous bull-embracing sport, is held in villages around Madurai and across the state.
  • Friday, 22 January — Thai Pusam (தைப்பூசம்)
    Dedicated to Lord Murugan, with kavadi processions at Palani and Chennai's Vadapalani temple — and most famously at Batu Caves in Malaysia, where devotees gather in enormous numbers. Full Thai Pusam 2027 guide →

Government Holidays in January 2027

The Tamil Nadu government typically declares Pongal (15 January), Thiruvalluvar Day (16 January) and Uzhavar Thirunal (17 January) as holidays, creating a long harvest-weekend break. Republic Day on Tuesday, 26 January is a national holiday across India. The official 2027 gazette should be confirmed when published; schools and banks usually follow the state list, while private offices often close only for the Pongal cluster. Long-distance buses and trains are heavily booked in the days before Pongal as city workers travel home to their villages.

Auspicious Days and Notes for January 2027

With Thai beginning on the 15th, the second half of January is traditionally considered a fresh, positive start. Thai is regarded as an auspicious month for weddings and new ventures, and families often schedule engagements and discussions about marriages in the weeks after Pongal. Margazhi bhajans and early-morning temple visits, which run all month until the 14th, conclude with the Bhogi celebrations, and in December-capable towns like Chennai the season's music festivals wind down at the same time.

What the Tamil Months Mean

January straddles two Tamil months. Margazhi (மார்கழி), until the 14th, evokes the "right path" — the month of pre-dawn bhajans and Chennai's music season. Thai (தை), beginning on Pongal day, means "pure" or "fresh" and is among the year's most auspicious stretches.

Festival Customs and Food

Bhogi ends Margazhi with a dawn bonfire of old mats, symbolising a clean start. Pongal is new rice boiled with jaggery and milk in a decorated clay pot until it overflows; families offer the first serving to the sun, then share ven pongal, sugarcane, turmeric and payasam. Mattu Pongal honours cattle with bathed, painted animals. On Thai Pusam, devotees carry kavadi to Murugan temples, often fasting until the evening puja.

Season and Farming

This is the tail end of the harvest. Sugarcane is cut for the Pongal pot and the season's first new rice reaches Tamil kitchens. Fields are assessed after the north-east monsoon and farmers plan the next sowing cycle while the weather is still cool. Chennai's markets fill with cane, jaggery and turmeric, and mild mornings suit the pre-dawn temple queues of Margazhi.

Travel and Temple Notes

Pongal is one of the heaviest travel periods of the year: book buses and trains to Tamil Nadu towns well ahead, and expect crowds in Madurai, Palani and Tiruvannamalai. Margazhi temple visits begin before sunrise, so dress warmly. On Thai Pusam, allow for processions and road diversions around Vadapalani, Palani and Batu Caves. Remove footwear where indicated.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

Those born after the Thai transition fall under Makara (Capricorn, மகரம்) in the solar-rashi system — patient, disciplined and dependable, with a practical approach to money and family duty. The Margazhi half belongs to Dhanu (Sagittarius, தனுசு), associated with optimism and curiosity. These are broad readings; the birth star matters more in a full horoscope.
