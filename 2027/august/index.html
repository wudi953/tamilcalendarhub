Festivals in August 2027

  • Tuesday, 3 August — Aadi Perukku (ஆடிப் பெருக்கு)
    The 18th day of Aadi, when rivers swell with monsoon rain. Families gather on riverbanks — the Cauvery above all — to offer rice dishes, flowers and lamps to the water, praying for a year of abundance. Full Aadi Perukku 2027 guide →
  • Mid-to-late August — Aavani observances
    As Aavani begins around the 17th, many families observe Varalakshmi Vratam on a Friday and the upakarma ceremonies for Vedic students; exact dates are set by the panchangam each year, so check temple notices for 2027.

Government Holidays in August 2027

Independence Day falls on Sunday, 15 August 2027 — a national holiday, with flag hoisting at schools, offices and public buildings; since it lands on a weekend, many organisations will not add a substitute day off. Aadi Perukku itself is not a general public holiday, though families in river towns often take the day for the riverside rituals. Confirm the state gazette for any local holiday announcements.

Auspicious Days and Notes for August 2027

Aadi Perukku is one of the auspicious moments of the Aadi month — riverside families treat it as a day of new starts for farming and business, and it is a popular date for engagements in the Cauvery delta. Once Aavani begins around the 17th, muhurtham activity picks up again for weddings in the months ahead. See our muhurtham dates guide for how timings are chosen.

What the Tamil Months Mean

August runs from Aadi (ஆடி), the monsoon month of beginnings, into Aavani (ஆவணி) around the 17th. Aavani is connected with the word for earth and with the Sravana nakshatra; it is a month of settled rains and growing crops. Alongside the Tamil calendar, August is also when the neighbouring Kerala celebration of Onam falls, and the shared cultural overlap is visible in markets and temple towns near the border.

Festival Customs and Food

Vinayaka Chathurthi, on the fourth day of Aavani, is the month's major festival. Families shape a clay Ganesha at home, decorate him with flowers and leaves, offer modak — the steamed rice-and-jaggery dumpling said to be his favourite — and worship before immersing the idol in a tank or the sea after a few days. Temples organise processions and annadanam. Payasam, sundal and banana-leaf meals are common prasad, and households avoid onion and garlic for the observance. The Onam sadya, a wide vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf, is a highlight in Kerala-border areas.

Season and Farming

The monsoon is established and the young paddy stands in the fields. Waterlogging and drainage matter as much as rainfall now, and farmers inspect bunds after every heavy shower. Nurseries are transplanted where the rains were late, and tank irrigation is coordinated across villages. Livestock are healthier with fresh grazing, and the market is full of greens, gourds and rain-fed vegetables. It is a month of steady growth rather than a single harvest — the work is continuous and the reward comes later in the year.

Travel and Temple Notes

Vinayaka Chathurthi processions and immersions can close roads in Chennai, Coimbatore and temple towns for an evening, so plan around them rather than through them. If you are carrying a home idol to the sea or a tank, go early and follow the designated immersion point. Once Aavani begins, wedding and muhurtham bookings pick up again after the cautious Aadi weeks, so priests and halls are in demand. Rain is frequent — keep footwear and documents protected, and allow extra travel time on city roads.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

Babies born after the Aavani transition fall under Simha (Leo, சிம்மம்), a rashi traditionally associated with confidence, generosity and a natural pull towards leadership. Simha natives are often described as warm-hearted and visible — people who like to be recognised and who protect their own. The Aadi portion belongs to Kataka (Cancer), associated with sensitivity and family feeling. These solar-rashi sketches are general by nature; the birth nakshatra and the rest of the chart provide the real detail.
