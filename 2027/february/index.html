Tamil Calendar 2027 February

Festivals in February 2027

  • Saturday, 20 February — Masi Magam (மாசி மகம்)
    Masi Magam falls on the day the Magam star aligns with the full moon in the Tamil month of Masi. Temple deities are carried in procession to the seashore, a river or a temple tank, and devotees take a ritual dip alongside them. The famous Mahamaham tank at Kumbakonam is closely associated with Masi celebrations, and coastal towns from Chennai to Rameswaram hold sea-bathing processions. Families believe the bath washes away accumulated negativity, and offerings are made to ancestors on this day. In Sri Lanka, Singapore and Malaysia, Murugan and Shiva temples hold parallel ceremonies, so diaspora families often plan temple visits around this weekend. Arrive early — processions usually begin in the morning and conclude around midday.

Government Holidays in February 2027

February 2027 has no major public holidays in Tamil Nadu — it is one of the calmest months of the year for work and school schedules. Banks and offices follow normal hours throughout. If you are planning temple visits for Masi Magam on Saturday the 20th, no leave is needed since it falls on a weekend, though crowds at popular coastal temples will be significant from early morning.

Auspicious Days and Notes for February 2027

Masi is traditionally associated with Shiva worship — the great Masi legend links the month to Shiva's appearance as a column of fire. Devotees often observe Shiva-related vows on Mondays of the month, and temple abhishekams are common. Because Thai ends around the 13th, the first half of February still carries the auspicious momentum of Thai, which many families use for engagements and property registrations before Masi begins. What the Tamil Months Mean

February begins in Thai (தை), the "fresh" month that opened with Pongal, and moves into Masi (மாசி) around the 14th. Masi is linked in folk etymology to the moon and to the waning half of the lunar month; it is a quieter, more reflective stretch than the festival-heavy weeks before it. In practical terms this is the coolest part of the year in Tamil Nadu, when mornings are misty in the interior and the temple calendar slows down before the spring festivals of Panguni.

Festival Customs and Food

The month's main observance is Masi Magam, when devotees gather at riverbanks, temple tanks and the sea for a ceremonial bath under the Magham star. Families bring flowers and newly cooked rice; in Kumbakonam the related Mahamaham draws pilgrims from across the state once every twelve years. Some temples take the deity out on a decorated raft at dusk. Food is simple — pongal, curd rice, dosa and payasam — and many fast until the evening puja.

Season and Farming

Fields are in the post-harvest lull. Farmers repair bunds and irrigation channels, check groundwater and prepare seed beds for the short second crop. This is the coolest stretch of the year, favoured for temple car festivals and outdoor work that is impossible in the heat of May. In the Cauvery delta, the weeks around Masi Magam coincide with a noticeable drop in river level.

Travel and Temple Notes

With the Pongal rush over, February is a comfortable month for temple travel: fewer pilgrims than in January, and pleasant weather for long queues and processions. Kumbakonam is the exception during Mahamaham years, when accommodation should be booked months ahead. Masi Magam draws crowds to Adyar and Marina in Chennai and to temple tanks across the state, so arrive early if you want a place at the water's edge. Carry a change of clothes and a towel for the ritual bath, and expect roads near the river to be closed to traffic until the procession ends.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

After the Masi transition, babies are associated with Kumbha (Aquarius, கும்பம்) — independent, original and concerned with the wider community. Kumbha natives are often described as thoughtful and unconventional. The Thai portion falls under Makara (Capricorn), associated with patience. The birth nakshatra and full chart carry more weight than the solar sign alone.
