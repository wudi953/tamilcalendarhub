Festivals in September 2027

  • Saturday, 4 September — Vinayagar Chathurthi (விநாயகர் சதுர்த்தி)
    The birthday of Lord Ganesha. Clay Vinayagar idols are installed in homes and street pandals, worshipped through the day with kozhukattai and ellu urundai, and later immersed in water. Neighbourhood processions run through the evening. Full Vinayagar Chathurthi 2027 guide →
  • Wednesday, 30 September — Navarathiri begins (நவராத்திரி)
    The nine-night festival of the Goddess starts on this day, running through 8 October with Vijayadashami on 9 October. Families set up the Golu display of dolls and invite guests for sundal and thamboolam. Full Navratri 2027 guide →

Government Holidays in September 2027

Vinayagar Chathurthi on Saturday, 4 September is expected to be a Tamil Nadu government holiday; falling on a Saturday, many organisations treat it as a normal weekend. Navarathiri's start on 30 September is not a holiday by itself. Confirm the final state gazette when published.

Auspicious Days and Notes for September 2027

Purattasi is traditionally a month of Venkateswara worship — many devotees observe Purattasi Saturdays with vegetarian food and special offerings, and some fast through the month. Because of these observances, Purattasi is generally avoided for weddings, while the Aavani days before mid-September carry the year's wedding-season momentum. What the Tamil Months Mean

September begins in Aavani (ஆவணி), the earth month of settled rains, and enters Purattasi (புரட்டாசி) around the 17th. Purattasi is the month of Navaratri, and its name is popularly connected with steadiness and auspiciousness. It is one of the most festival-dense stretches of the Tamil year, when homes, temples and streets are decorated for nine nights and the air is full of music, lamps and the sound of devotees singing.

Festival Customs and Food

Navaratri is observed over nine nights with golu — tiered displays of dolls and miniature scenes — in homes, and with classical music and dance programmes in temples. Each evening brings visitors bearing sundal, a seasoned legume dish, and the host returns a small portion as prasad. The ninth day, Vijayadashami, marks the victory of good over evil; children begin their first letters in a vidyarambam ceremony, and tools and vehicles are worshipped in Ayudha Puja. Payasam, dosa and offerings of fruit are common across the festival.

Season and Farming

The heavy rains ease, but the fields remain full and the paddy is steadily maturing. Farmers spend the month weeding, repairing bunds and monitoring water levels rather than sowing. The post-monsoon green is at its best, and vegetables from rain-fed farms are abundant and cheap in local markets. This is also a practical pause before the harvest: livestock are moved to higher ground after floods, and storage is checked in preparation for the grain that will come in over the next two months.

Travel and Temple Notes

Navaratri is one of the busiest temple seasons of the year. Expect long queues in the evenings, especially at Amman temples, and road closures around major processions on Vijayadashami. If you are planning a vidyarambam or a new venture on the final day, confirm the auspicious timing with a priest and book well in advance — this is a peak muhurtham date. Golu-open homes are welcoming but crowded; remove footwear and ask before photographing the display. Carry an umbrella for the lingering showers.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

Those born after the Purattasi transition belong to Kanya (Virgo, கன்னி), a rashi traditionally described as careful, analytical and service-minded. Kanya natives are often said to notice details others miss, to keep order and to take pride in being useful — traits that suit a month of meticulous golu arrangements and ritual precision. The Aavani days earlier in the month fall under Simha (Leo), associated with confidence and warmth. As always, these are solar-sign generalisations; the birth star and moon carry the finer reading.
