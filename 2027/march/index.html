Tamil Calendar 2027 March

March 2027 is the last month of the old Tamil year. Panguni Uthiram on 22 March fills temples across Tamil Nadu, and the year winds down toward Puthandu on 14 April.

Festivals in March 2027

  • Monday, 22 March — Panguni Uthiram (பங்குனி உத்திரம்)
    Panguni Uthiram falls when the Uthiram nakshatra coincides with the full moon of Panguni, the final month of the Tamil year. It is one of the most important days for Murugan worship: temple chariots (ther) roll out at Palani, Thiruchendur and the six great abodes of Murugan, and devotees carry kavadi and paal kudam (milk pots) to fulfil vows. The day is also linked to the celestial weddings of Shiva–Parvati and Murugan–Devasena, so it is considered highly auspicious for marriage-related prayers. Murugan temples stay open from dawn; the evening processions are the highlight. If you plan to attend, reach early — parking near hill temples is restricted on the festival day.

Government Holidays in March 2027

March 2027 has no major Tamil Nadu public holidays, and no national holidays fall in the month. Banks, schools and offices follow their regular schedule. Panguni Uthiram on Monday, 22 March is a significant temple festival but is not a general government holiday; devotees typically take a day of personal leave to attend processions. Christian communities in Tamil Nadu observe Good Friday around late March in some years — check your local church calendar, as that date is announced separately from the Tamil almanac.

Auspicious Days and Notes for March 2027

The last two weeks of Panguni are the final days of the old Tamil year, and traditionally families complete pending religious vows before Puthandu. Panguni is considered a mixed month for new beginnings — many almanacs advise waiting for Chithirai for major launches, though Panguni Uthiram itself is auspicious for prayers related to marriage. If you are planning wedding dates for late 2027, start consultations now; see our muhurtham dates guide.

Note: all dates on this page follow Indian Standard Time (IST), calculated for Chennai — other regions may vary slightly. Always confirm with a local panchangam.

What the Tamil Months Mean

March carries the year from Masi into Panguni (பங்குனி) around the 14th or 15th. Panguni is named after the Uttaram star and is often glossed as the month of "pods" or new growth — a fitting description for a period when mango trees flower and the landscape begins to dry after the rains. It is the last month of the Tamil year; the cycle closes here before Puthandu opens a new one in mid-April. That position gives Panguni a sense of finishing and settling accounts before the fresh start.

Festival Customs and Food

Panguni Uthiram, when the Uthiram star meets the full moon, is one of the year's most favoured wedding days. Temples — above all the Meenakshi temple in Madurai — stage elaborate divine-wedding processions. Devotees fast, offer flowers and turmeric, and pray for marriage or children. Panguni also brings large kite-flying gatherings in Chennai as the winds pick up. Mango pachadi, pongal, sundal and payasam mark the season.

Season and Farming

This is the transition from cool to hot. Harvest work is largely done in the delta, and attention turns to mango orchards, where flowering and fruit setting need careful watering. Farmers prepare dry fields and watch for the first signs of the south-west monsoon. Outdoor work moves to early morning, and markets shift toward mangoes and tender coconut.

Travel and Temple Notes

Panguni Uthiram brings heavy traffic to Madurai and to major Murugan and Shiva temples, with processions that can close roads for hours. If you are planning a wedding-related visit, book rooms and priests early — this is one of the busiest muhurtham windows of the year. Chennai's kite season is family-friendly but crowded at the beach, so keep valuables close and heed local wind warnings. The heat is building, so carry water and a hat for daytime temple visits, and prefer the early-morning or evening slots for darshan.

Born This Month: Star and Temperament

Those born in the Panguni half are placed under Meena (Pisces, மீனம்) in the solar-rashi tradition. Meena is commonly described as imaginative, compassionate and receptive — a sign associated with water, intuition and the arts. It is also the final sign of the zodiac, so traditional readings link it with reflection and completion. The Masi portion of the month belongs to Kumbha (Aquarius), associated with independent thinking. These solar readings are broad; in a full horoscope the birth star and the position of the moon matter far more.
